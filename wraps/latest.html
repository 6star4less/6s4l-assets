Bidding for an Upgrade - What works and what doesn't
What the cruise lines won’t tell you
There is much written these days, especially by latter-day influencers, about supposedly 'proven techniques' to bag an upgrade. Of course, it's all done to drive clicks. Regardless of whether they are giving advice about upgrades on aircraft or cruise ships, the common thread of all such posts is the idea that if you are pleasant enough to the right person, mention it is your anniversary, or that your loyalty to their brand can tip the odds and score you a better seat or cabin.
After decades working on the front lines in both the aviation and cruise industries, I can categorically say that every single one of these ‘experts’ has no idea how upgrades are awarded, and their tips are as worthless as their compelling need for attention.
The evidence is clear. The upgrade fairy is dead. She was replaced by an algorithm. An algorithm that doesn’t give two hoots about how nice you might be, how loyal you are to the cruise line or in most cases how often you sail.
Here is what actually happens now, and how to work it.
