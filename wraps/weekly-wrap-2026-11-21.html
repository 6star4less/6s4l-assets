THE UPGRADE PROJECT
What 39 real upgrade bids are telling us
Last week I launched the bid tracker. Readers log what they bid for an upgrade, won or lost, and it all goes onto the tracker anonymously. No names, no ships, no sailing dates.
How it works: you tell me the category you booked, the one you bid for, what you offered and whether it won. The tracker turns every bid into a per person, per night figure, so a 7 night cruise and a 14 night cruise compare fairly. Leave your email and I send you a private link to mark it won or lost when you hear back.
•39 bids logged so far: 28 won, 6 lost and 5 still waiting. That is 82% of the bids that have heard back.
•Every one of the 10 bids to move up to Ocean view or Balcony won.
•Suites are where it gets interesting. A Regent bid of US$51 a night won a suite, while a bid of US$238 a night lost one. The amount matters less than how many suites are still empty.
•Azamara bidders have been the busiest, with 29 bids, thanks to the members of my Azamara group.
New this week: the form now asks how many nights, the currency, whether the amount was per person or for the cabin, and how many days before departure the offer arrived.

THE CABIN COUNTER
How many cabins are left on your ship?
The number of unsold cabins is the best guide to whether a fare is about to rise or fall. The Cabin Counter puts that number in your hands.
How it works: pick your voyage, and it checks the live booking system and counts the unsold cabins, category by category. The result lands in your inbox, usually within minutes. Every check is saved, so each time someone checks the same sailing you can see which way it is moving, and every cruise you check goes onto your own wish list.
A few from recent checks:
•Regent Seven Seas Grandeur, 23 February 2027: just 3 balcony suites left, about 1% of them.
•Explora III, 20 December 2026: 3 balcony suites left, about 1%.
•Seabourn Pursuit, 24 May 2027: inside and ocean view sold out, and the balcony fare up US$300 as cabins sold.
•Azamara Pursuit, 11 March 2027: two cabins came back since 6 November and the inside fare dropped US$450.
Counts are a guide only. Lines hold cabins back and some are waiting on payments.
